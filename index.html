Research direction

My path into research began with a simple question about the materials we build with: why do structures meant to last so often need repair? Self-healing concrete drew me in because it offers a way to address cracking, extend durability and reduce the need for repair and new material. More broadly, I am interested in smart materials that respond to damage on their own.

Machine learning entered my work through collaboration with Dr. Fariborz Tehrani’s research group. There I saw how data-driven models can predict durability, support service-life estimation and help reduce cement content in mix design. Today my experimental work on self-healing and my modelling work inform each other.

After my M.Sc., I hope to continue as a doctoral researcher working toward more durable, lower-carbon construction materials. I am open to experimental and computational approaches, and interested in joining a group where I can learn and contribute.
