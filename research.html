Master’s thesis

Effect of silica nanoparticles on self-healing concrete incorporating lightweight expanded clay aggregates saturated with sodium silicate

Supervisors: Dr. Sadegh Dardaei, Tarbiat Modares University, and Dr. Fariborz M. Tehrani, California State University, Fresno.

My thesis investigates whether silica nanoparticles improve crack healing in concrete containing slag and lightweight expanded clay aggregates saturated with sodium silicate. The aggregates carry the healing agent, while the study examines its interaction with nanosilica and the potential formation of additional calcium silicate hydrate (C–S–H). The experimental programme compares mixes with different nanosilica contents, induces cracks and evaluates healing and mechanical performance. This is an ongoing M.Sc. project; degree completion is expected in May 2027.

Inside my master’s thesis

PVA-coated LECA as a sodium silicate carrier

Lightweight expanded clay aggregate (LECA) serves as a porous reservoir for the sodium-silicate-based healing agent. After impregnation, a polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) coating is applied to the particle surface to help limit premature leakage during concrete mixing and placement.

For preparation, LECA particles were immersed in a 40% sodium silicate solution for 3 days and brought to a saturated surface-dry (SSD) condition. A 5% PVA solution was then sprayed onto the impregnated particles, followed by warm-air drying to form a protective polymer coating. The mass gain after impregnation was approximately 11.98% relative to the initial dry mass, measured at SSD condition.

Fig. 1. Actual laboratory photographs of dry LECA particles and particles after sodium silicate impregnation and PVA coating. The photographs show the documented initial and final conditions; no separate photograph of the intermediate, uncoated impregnated state is presented.

When a crack intersects a coated particle, damage to the PVA film is expected to release the stored sodium silicate into the surrounding cementitious matrix. The released silicate can react with calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂), promoting secondary C–S–H formation within the crack and potentially contributing to crack sealing.

Conference contribution

Machine Learning for Low-Carbon Concrete

Alireza Shirazi, Ali A. Shakeri, Nikta Hasaninasab, Sadegh Dardaei, Fariborz M. Tehrani

Transportation Research Board Annual Meeting · TRB 2026

› Abstract

This research proposes a data-driven machine learning approach aimed at optimizing the cement content in concrete used for transportation infrastructure, such as roads, bridges, and retaining walls. The production of cement is a significant contributor to CO₂ emissions and increasing costs in construction projects. By reducing cement content using optimized multi-component mixtures, this method tackles both ecological and performance requirements of transportation structures. A comprehensive dataset of concrete mix designs, which includes supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs) and nanomaterials, was compiled to support the decision-making process. A hybrid machine learning model that combines Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Genetic Algorithms (GA), and XGBoost was developed to predict compressive strength and determine the best mix combinations for high-load applications, like pavements and bridge decks. To better handle incomplete and diverse data, a binary-feature masking normalization technique was introduced, which improves model accuracy and reliability. The proposed framework effectively identifies concrete mixes that significantly decrease cement usage while maintaining structural strength. The use of SCMs such as fly ash, silica fume, and metakaolin, along with nanomaterials like carbon nanotubes and nanoclay, showed synergistic benefits in enhancing strength and durability, critical factors for enduring transportation infrastructure. This study underscores the promise of AI-driven material design in advancing low-carbon, high performance concrete solutions for roads and bridges, supporting global sustainability objectives and enhancing infrastructure resilience.

Conference contribution

Towards Green Concrete: A Data-Driven Machine Learning for Reducing Cement Content

Alireza Shirazi, Ali A. Shakeri, Nikta Hasaninasab, Sadegh Dardaei, Fariborz M. Tehrani

14th International Congress on Civil Engineering · 21–23 October 2025, Sharif University of Technology, Tehran

Conference paper record ↗ · Conference website ↗
› Abstract

This paper introduces an innovative data-driven approach to designing green concrete by minimizing cement content through the optimization of multi-component blends. A comprehensive dataset of 2,332 experimental concrete mix designs incorporating supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs) and nanomaterials was compiled from 31 scientific publications. A hybrid machine learning model combining Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Genetic Algorithms (GA), and XGBoost was developed to predict compressive strength and identify optimal mix compositions. To address the challenge of incomplete data, a binary-feature masking normalization strategy was implemented, allowing the model to process heterogeneous datasets with improved accuracy and robustness. The proposed framework successfully identifies sustainable concrete mixes that reduce cement consumption by up to 40% without compromising mechanical performance. Hybrid combinations of SCMs such as fly ash, silica fume, and metakaolin, alongside nanomaterials like carbon nanotubes and nanoclay, demonstrated synergistic effects in enhancing strength while lowering environmental impact. The results confirm that intelligent integration of AI techniques with material science can significantly advance the development of high-performance, low-carbon concrete, offering a scalable solution for the construction industry's sustainability goals.

SubmittedSelf-Healing Concrete: A Review of Healing Mechanisms, Durability, Performance, and Implementation Challenges+

Alireza Shirazi, Ali A. Shakeri, Sadegh Dardaei

Submitted to Journal of Civil Engineering and Materials Application

› Abstract

Cracks in concrete provide pathways for water and aggressive substances, accelerating deterioration and compromising long-term durability. Self-healing concrete (SHC) has therefore attracted considerable research interest as a material system that can autonomously reduce crack damage and potentially extend structural service life. This review systematically surveys the principal self-healing mechanisms and evaluates their potential for practical application. This review examines autogenous healing mechanisms based on continued cement hydration and calcium carbonate precipitation alongside enhanced systems incorporating mineral and crystalline admixtures, fibers, superabsorbent polymers, and lightweight aggregates. The review also covers autonomous approaches, including microcapsules, vascular networks, bacteria-based systems, electrochemical healing, and shape-memory alloys. It pays particular attention to the relationship between crack-healing capability and durability, as well as the recovery of mechanical and transport-related properties. In addition, the review discusses the practical viability of different strategies in terms of cost, long-term durability, construction compatibility, and potential for repeated use. The review identifies major barriers to large-scale implementation, including environmental variability, limited long-term validation, inconsistent evaluation methods, and the lack of standardized design guidelines. The review then outlines future research priorities involving hybrid systems, life-cycle assessment, standardized testing, and machine-learning-assisted design.
